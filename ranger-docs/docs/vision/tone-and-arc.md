# Tone & Story Arc

## The shape

```
wholesome ─────────┐
                   │
                   └──── twist ──── dark, fast, unexpected ────▶
```

The opening is deliberately, almost suspiciously wholesome. Standard Pokémon Ranger fare:

- You're a recruit at a Ranger training school
- Your first missions are rescuing pets, helping hikers, clearing routes of stray Pokémon
- The mentor figures are warm. The other recruits are friendly rivals. The world feels safe.
- Stakes are low and personal — a lost Skitty, a flooded campsite, a kid who needs help finding their way home.

This is not a fakeout — it's a *foundation*. The wholesomeness has to be earned and felt for the turn to land.

## The turn

Somewhere in mid-Act 1 or early Act 2 (TBD), something happens that recontextualises everything. Possible shapes (not yet chosen):

- A routine mission goes catastrophically wrong
- A trusted figure is revealed to be compromised
- Something the player has been told is impossible turns out to be both possible and already happening
- A wholesome NPC dies in a way that the game doesn't soften

After the turn, the tone shifts. Not edgy-for-the-sake-of-it — the game retains its warmth in places — but the stakes are real, the threats are credible, and the player learns that the institution they joined is operating in a world more dangerous than the training program let on.

## What this asks of the design

- **Early game must be genuinely good** even taken on its own merits. If it reads as filler before "the real game", the contrast fails.
- **The turn must be unsignaled** in marketing, in the opening hours, and in the early game's tone. No foreshadowing winks to camera.
- **Post-turn content must earn the shift** — the darker material has to be handled with care, not used as shock value.
